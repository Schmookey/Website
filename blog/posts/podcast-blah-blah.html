Podcast Blah Blah

UPDATED Wed 1 Apr 2026 16:20 BST
Wed 1 Apr 2026 16:20 BST
⏱ – min read 💬 0 mentions ❤️ 0 🔁 0 🔗 0 replies

I have been listening to podcasts now for the past 20 years, give or take. It all started with an iPod Classic and finding my favourite weekend radio show was releasing their content without the music — weird concept, right? Now, I liked the music on their show — very indie, very rock — a lot of great bands on the cusp who sadly would never actually make it. However, it felt like a novel idea that I didn't need to listen to their 3-hour show; I could listen to an 80–90 minute "podcast" on the bus, train, or walking to and from work.

You would try and explain them to people and they would just look confused. For me, BBC Radio and NPR were the first great resources, and it was as true then as now that public broadcasters are the most willing to take risks on new "things" — to be ahead of the curve. I also remember having an "iPlayer" day every Monday where I would watch content on BBC iPlayer through my PlayStation 3. This was a great thing because I found things like BBC Click and Storyville.

Podcasts were a way to listen to shows that weren't "cool" shows like 'Just a Minute' and look like a weirdo gut-laughing to Adam and Joe on public transport. The spoken-word medium has a knack for making you feel like one of a select few who get to listen or know about it, where the reality is they were cultivating a massive audience. Love him or hate him, Joe Rogan commands a massive fanbase and arguably had a say in more than one U.S. election — yes, I do think that's the peak. Due to the lockdowns of 2020, many comedians in the UK were told they had two choices: write a book or make a podcast... heck, most of TV was basically a video podcast during this period.

Your average U.S. talk show just looked like a mid-level podcast setup. I think most production companies realised they have a good stable of talent — let's have them present shows, and we have a ready-made cast of characters to guest those shows. Put them on YouTube, Spotify, and the like, bypass the broadcaster, and keep the income for yourself. While Joe Rogan is at the peak, I think what comes next is oversaturation. I remember when there were five terrestrial TV channels in the UK; now there are so many choices, and yet it can still feel like there is nothing on. With the advent of streaming services, everyone was happy to pay one company £9.99 a month for access to basically everything, and now there are so many streamers and paywalls that people just can't afford it, so don't watch anything. There are like 1,500 football podcasts, 3,000 comedians and their rotating guests, and all your fan-made content too.

I don't fault the content — you can throw so much against a wall and you would be surprised at what sticks. The general public are genuinely fickle — they like what they like for no rhyme or reason. There are genuinely some good podcasts/vodcasts out there. We are in a sweet spot, particularly in the UK, where we can get these podcasts for free through places like YouTube, and they have paid-for extra content like "The Rest Is..." Goalhanger shows do. How long until you have to subscribe to listen to them all, though? If and when someone gets that bright idea — and especially if it works for one — they will all try it.

I like the BBC's idea about creating a British streaming service — a one-for-all for the likes of the BBC, ITV, Channel 4, and Channel 5. Indeed, this is what BritBox was ultimately supposed to be — like Hulu in the U.S. I think it would be great for the creative industries in the UK if they had this for television, radio, and podcasts alike.

I made a podcast for about five minutes a few years ago, and I have been part of others. I've always said that I feel podcasts are the place we can slow down — this is the place where we do have time. How much time? As much as you want! I've taken the idea that it's a great place for discussion and fleshing out ideas and arguments — not your boomer soundbites on the news. Michelle Obama's famous line is "when they go low, we go high"; mine was "if they are tabloid, we are broadsheet". Audio is a great medium because anything can happen — there is nothing stopping the imagination from putting pictures to those words.

Right now, my podcast app of choice is AntennaPod. I found it at the right time. A lot of the good podcast players were now charging for quite basic things or including features that felt irrelevant, like file storage, etc. I was becoming more disillusioned with tech in general, so I decided to see if there was any free open-source software I could find and maybe switch fully over to. The AntennaPod app was initially a bit buggy to begin with, but it has really been doing great with each passing update — people who clearly love podcasts looking to make a great app and not make money from it.

Lots of my viewing seems to be done on YouTube these days. I like that some weekend mornings I can wake up and watch J-League football from Japan. I also like that television shows such as the Mock the Week reboot are released online and on their new TV channel — I am more likely to watch it on YouTube because of fewer clicks, I think. I went from watching British basketball all the time when they broadcast exclusively on YouTube to hardly ever since they started primarily broadcasting on DAZN. Some of the bigger basketball matches do get broadcast on YouTube; however, Caledonia Gladiators barely get a look in. I am happy to watch a podcast these days like Rest Is Entertainment or Pablo Torre Finds Out — there is no real difference between watching or listening other than one has visuals. It's not like Torre is juggling fruit. I think it's just a weird preference thing that I will watch some but only listen to others when their content is just sitting and talking.

Podcasts are a great example of how nowadays you just need to make content. I remember my lecturer telling us this at college all those years ago when he was showing us a Zoom recorder — one that could also film people. He was talking about how tech was getting better and cheaper all the time. I still have my own audio-only Zoom recorder — expensive, yes, but it paid for itself very quickly. The barrier to entry in the "mediasphere" was already much less expensive, and yet we didn't see TikTok, Twitch, or even what YouTube was to become. Genuinely funny people can now just be funny and end up on shows like SNL UK. I think now that your average celebrity has a podcast of their own and there are big stables even here in the UK, we are at oversaturation. I feel podcasting has already reached its heights; if there are any real paywalls for the content alone, then people will just stop listening. Having them on easy-to-access spaces like YouTube will keep them around for some time, and as long as there are smart people willing to take the time to talk about smart things, people being funny, or there are interesting stories to be told, podcasts will still be here.
