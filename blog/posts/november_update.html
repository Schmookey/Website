    November Update

    UPDATED Sun 15 November 2026 12:00
    2026-11-15 12:00 GMT
    ⏱ – min read 💬 0 mentions ❤️ 0 🔁 0 🔗 0 replies

    I reckon November is the weirdest month of the year in some respects. You have plenty going on; here, we had Halloween and then Bonfire Night pretty much within the same week. It's after Bonfire Night that you start to feel that anticipation towards Christmas. In my case, it's a feeling of dread. Meeting the family is fine, and exchanging presents is alright, but it's just the whirlwind of the week and being off schedule. You know the date – just not the day of the week. All your favourite TV shows suddenly air their Christmas specials, and even the random daytime telly gets in on the spirit. On a side note, Christmas TV is filmed long before it's aired. I can't imagine it ever gets easier pretending it's Christmas in April, hoping and praying none of the guests do anything stupid or get caught up in some legal trouble before December.

    I find myself seeking some sort of solace in my blog again, more as therapy, to just blah blah blah as the words are typed onto the screen (I reckon a new keyboard and mouse could be my Black Friday gift to myself). I am quite happy with some of the aesthetics now. I like that I have been able to sort out music and TV journals. I like giving a snapshot of what I am watching and listening to, albeit it's curated. It's curated so I can force myself to listen to and watch different things. The latest book I'm reading during any spare time is Walking Practice by Dolki Min. It's been wild so far. It's about an alien who landed on Earth and has to find their way to survive. It's a wild ride. I am unashamedly vibes-based.

    I read the blurb, but no more beforehand, as I like to be surprised by the story. The book felt so female-coded initially; I heard the voice of the narrator in my head as a woman. When reading more about the author, I realised they were male, and I was genuinely surprised by my assumption. However, it's also a brilliant sleight of hand by the author. They use this alongside transgender coding to really push the fact that the alien has no gender in the human sense; they can be either male or female depending on the situation. The main character is almost childlike in how self-centred they are and how they can swing from love one minute to "I will murder you" the next. The quote below was my first proper belly laugh in the book, and it set the tone. For context, they are tired, have travelled to get there, and the lift to the other person's apartment is broken.

    I also added the music function to my vibe-coded multimedia Linux app. It works really well. I was able to get it working with Linux's MPRIS. Basically, it will show the image of my music or audiobook and provide media functions like play, pause and volume controls when the screen is locked. Adding the files feels a bit clunky. I think when I return to it, I'll look into being able to drag and drop the files and whether there is an offline way to create playlists and/or save them. I think once the music component is working in a way I like, I might look at either a PDF function or video function. I am happy with just the EPUB, audiobook and music functions as it is, to be honest, so it might also be the right thing to leave it at that and refine it more.

    I have also carried out various tweaks to the website and some background SEO/webmaster shenanigans. I also added a trading-card-style profile to the About page. The trading card has clickable links to my other spaces in the Fediverse. I don't really know if the trading card will stay, but I like its interactivity with Fediverse links and microformats, and the ability to make use of so much information in a unique way. I am thinking of more ideas with the website. More of me chatting seems kind of meh, so maybe some short-form reviews of things I've read or watched, and interesting IndieWeb or Fediverse content.

    I didn't technically go to a fireworks display this year; in a sense, one came to me. I was working late into the night so couldn't attend, but it happened that the big event was happening across from my house. Doggo was not best pleased. That being said, I was able to witness the roaring bonfire and excellent fireworks display. Kai, my doggo, has been unwell recently. I think the fireworks really did a number on his nerves, so I've been keeping an eye on him and giving him extra care and attention. He seems to be coming through the other side now.

    My TV habits over the past month or so have been Mock the Week, Have You Been Paying Attention? and Celebrity Traitors UK. Wow, Traitors was so much fun to watch. It started off slow, or more accurately, the Richard E. Grant situation felt like its own mini drama, like when a film does a prequel. I loved the Traitor picks this season, though: Maya Jama, proving she will be a television mainstay for years to come; James Acaster – the reluctant Traitor making old Frank Sinatra proud by doing it his way – and King Kenny. He's from the YouTube/TikTok generation of "celebs". I certainly didn't know who he was when he was announced as a contestant. He's knocked it out of the park. I can see him fronting a TV show for young adults on Channel 4 or maybe a slot on BBC Radio 1 or 1Xtra.

    The drama was there until the end, though. In the final, Kenny got caught out by wanting to stop the voting with four contestants left – it guaranteed he would be voted off. James Blunt misread it in the last three, and he wanted to keep voting when Romesh and Bash wanted to end the game. He sealed his fate with that. His face when he worked out what he'd done. Genuine frustration. I love that Romesh and Bash won. It was a victory for the Faithfuls! I also love that both their charities of choice were for refugees. Absolute right-wing bellends have been villainising refugees over the past couple of years in a blatant attempt at "hey, look over there!" trying to get everyone to look away from the train wreck they themselves have caused with their lazy nativism and ill-thought-out politics. Whoops, I'm ranting...

    I have also been enjoying the second season of the Scrubs reboot. I love that the focus is still on JD, Turk and Elliot, and it's not just the JD show. The writing has brought out the best of each of the characters whilst also showcasing the younger interns. Two words, though: Nurse Dubois. He's been the glue in the series, and it's been an absolute delight to see the character develop. I've been such a nerd, too, watching their 'making of' shows after each episode.

    My podcasts lately have all been political to some degree or another because of the US midterms. Yes, they affect us too. Blue-nami indeed. That said, in the US, we have seen this kind of decisive victory before, only for the politicians to be voted out soon after. In the UK, we are used to having a political party ruling for 10+ years and winning successive elections. Will the Democrats get three terms and two presidents, or will it just be another flash in the pan? There is one absolute certainty: they need literal root-and-branch structural change if they wish to even try and keep hold of their modern empire.

    On a lighter note, sometimes you don't know what you have until it's gone. My toaster went on the blink recently; I just ignored it and decided to wait for a reason to get a new one. Well, my hot water dispenser broke this week. This was my sign: I ordered a new toaster and a kettle on Amazon for next-day delivery. As much as I wasn't missing toast, when I had a slice with peanut butter from the new toaster, it was like meeting an old friend again.
