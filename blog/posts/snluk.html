My Thoughts on SNL UK

UPDATED Tue 24 March 2026 22:30
Tue 24 March 2026 22:30 GMT
⏱ – min read 💬 0 ❤️ 0 🔁 0 🔗 0 replies

This weekend, the first episode of the British Saturday Night Live aired. There was an SNL-like show in the 80s called Friday Night Live, before being retitled Saturday Live. There were some elements of SNL in the show, but there were also significant differences. The show was a launchpad for the likes of TV’s Craig Ferguson, Craig Charles, who later went on to Red Dwarf, and Tracey Ullman, who would ultimately introduce us to The Simpsons. The show helped boost the careers of many of this country’s comedy heavyweights.

They have tried to air the US SNL in the UK before on ITV2, but it didn’t gain traction. Most people know of SNL not for the full shows, but because of their ‘Digital Shorts’ and when the production would clip the show’s sketches for YouTube. My first proper experience was Andy Samberg’s Digital Short ‘People Getting Punched Right Before Eating’. It’s dumb internet humour, but also an example of humour telling a great story in three minutes.

In the run-up to the show, it felt like they were getting more airtime and interest in the USA than here. While I understand that, it felt like it could be a bad omen. Their UK broadcaster, BSkyB, doesn’t really get the same kind of viewership as the BBC or ITV, but in this day and age it’s all about the clicks. They will make money through social media and gain bigger notoriety. I do like that they’ve skipped a generation and gone with younger talent more suited to TikTok and online virality. It means they will have a better understanding of their potential audience. Also, if this thing pays off, Sky could be raking it in for years—if Larry Dean turns out to be the next Eddie Murphy or Ayoade Bamgboye the next Tina Fey. There are just a few names at the top of this post regarding the cast of the Saturday Live format, and they would be classed as “lesser known” when you look at their full cast list on Wikipedia. This cast could be front and centre for the Comcast-owned Sky schedules for years to come. When you look at that, you’d take those odds: fail and, meh, well, we tried—or succeed, and succeed massively.

I watched the first episode on Sunday (yes, I know, I know...). The cold open was very strong and timely—very well written—and honestly captured Keir Starmer perfectly. I would call the Prime Minister beige, but that would be an insult to the colour, honestly. The only thing that kind of irked me was George Fouracres constantly referring to Hammed Animashaun, who was playing our Deputy Prime Minister, as Lammy. I totally get it, though, because despite David Lammy being in Parliament since Tony Blair was in power, he is far less known and has the charisma of a cabbage. The show was, on the whole, very funny. It was biting when it needed to be, particularly during Weekend Update. Jack Shep as Diana, though—what a meme! Absolutely scene-stealing, and he barely had to do anything.

Another show that’s crossed the pond—but from Britain this time—is Have I Got News For You, a show that is firmly part of the British comedy furniture. In a lot of respects, the show works in the UK because you have the working-class Paul Merton on one team and the upper-class Ian Hislop on the other. It plays to our class system, but works well when they gang up on that week’s special guest host. They have tried to produce the show in America before, but if you watch clips of it on YouTube, it’s too similar to the UK version—even with the host doing a similar cadence to a lot of the British hosts. This modern version of HIGNFY US has the UK production behind it. They have picked an excellent host in Roy Wood Jr—a host with his own timing and cadence—he’s made it his own. Amber Ruffin on Hislop’s side of the desk—that lady is just funny; she oozes comedy. Michael Ian Black, on Paul’s side, does a great job of keeping the show somewhat grounded while making himself the butt of the jokes. The only bizarre thing about it is that it seems to fit well on CNN, a news network. They satirise the news on a real news network; although, to be fair, is there such a thing as satire these days?

From a personal standpoint, I’ve been really happy to see the return of Mock the Week—it’s been really funny. Maybe their four-year hiatus was a good thing, and maybe not having to be as impartial as their time on the BBC has helped. To account for the ad breaks they need now, the show lasts one hour with some extra rounds, and they have worked really well. The panel also seems a bit looser—or maybe that’s just the editing. A sign of the modern times is that I didn’t watch it on their new home, TLC—not once. The channel was smart enough to release the full shows on their YouTube channel.

I need to binge the second season of Last One Laughing UK on Amazon. I loved the first one—Bob Mortimer is a legend and deserves to defend his crown. If you get the chance, you should watch the Canadian version; it really is a veritable who’s who of Canadian comedy. You will get to hear about Tom Green’s delicious cheese sandwiches, and, well, Colin Mochrie is in it... need I say more?

I hope to see good things from SNL UK for the rest of this series, given the international press it’s had... If they win, British comedy wins.
