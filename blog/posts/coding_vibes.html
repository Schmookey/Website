Coding Vibes

UPDATED Fri 6 Nov 2026 21:30
2026-11-06 21:30 GMT
⏱ – min read 💬 0 mentions ❤️ 0 🔁 0 🔗 0 replies

Hello you! It's been a while, hasn't it? I know, I know, I just haven't had the same zest and zeal to really keep up to date with the website. Firstly, I will say I am no AI evangelist. Those people are the same people who told us Bitcoin was the future and NFTs are worth something. People finding new and interesting ways to part you from your money without offering a service. I do like that AI can be a great aid for jobs and people with disabilities – it shouldn't be seen as a replacement for people. I think about Jevons paradox. Higher demand and lower costs encourage wider adoption and greater overall demand. I don't think this is the end of jobs like coding in the workplace or even bedroom coders. I think it will only be the beginning.

Someone with an idea for an app or a fix for something can do it in a weekend instead of a week. People will be able to get through more jobs building apps, software and even SaaS products. People really don't need all the latest computers or the best AI plans. Most of these new products can be made with the help of Chinese AI models, which are really not that far behind their American equivalents, and they are far cheaper to run. Plus, don't discount the majority who are bumming it on OpenAI or Anthropic freemium plans.

I think we could potentially be entering a mini golden age of open-source programming alongside the supposed push to download Linux. It will be really interesting to see what comes out of it. There is open-source software like the Adobe alternative ArtCraft, which takes all your Adobe and Microsoft subscription products and offers a very close free version that you can download and keep rather than subscribe to.

There is also OccupySAAS, who seem very motivated by industry job losses and the rise of AI. They clearly state they don't steal any code and won't use any leaked information. They only use publicly available information. They are currently touting alternatives for Auth0 and Salesforce.

The idea of open-source alternatives is not new. LibreOffice, for one, that suite of Word, Excel and PowerPoint alternatives, has been around for some time. Audacity is another. It's basically an entry point for anyone learning to edit audio, and it's incredibly versatile. These are examples of good enough software that can do the majority of the work you need to do. Why aren't they widely used? I think it's the same reason you order a takeaway some nights instead of cooking.

Individuals and businesses use Microsoft 365 and Salesforce because they are so ubiquitous and they offer 24/7 support and 99% uptime. They just work – they're always there when you need them. You get a freshly cooked lamb shawarma delivered to your door. The food might be overpriced, and it is probably the same thing you always get, but it's convenient and there when you need it.

I also think that millions of applications will be built that never see any kind of public release, or if they do, it's just to show they exist rather than market them for other people's use. I've been on a bit of a kick for some time to purchase and own my own content. I've spoken about this previously on my website. While there are plenty of good ebook reader apps out there, I decided to try and build my own for my own bespoke needs.

I wanted something with a moveable sidebar to show chapters, an infinite scroll rather than pages, and a bar at the bottom of the app which shows the chapter number, page number and what percentage I have read of the chapter. I like the percentage bar as it acts like an incentive, whereas if I just saw how many pages I had left in the book overall, it would feel like a chore. The background is black and the text is white to reduce eye strain, and the little affectations like the chapter scroll bar and buttons are green.

Given that it was fairly straightforward to vibe code, I decided to add a separate audiobook function. There was a load of wrangling! I initially wanted to show the audiobook files separately from the audio player, but it wasn't really handling it well, so I chose to add the chapters below the playback bar, and it worked far better. I set it to save the reading and audiobook positions offline so you can continue where you left off. It was important that I added that function, or the app would be pointless.

My philosophy is DRM-free and offline only. I want to promote offline use and using content you've actually bought, rather than licensed content or breaking any Digital Rights Management agreements. I am toying with the idea of adding a music function, but I'm not sure I want it to become an everything app like ArtCraft have created with their versions of the Adobe and Microsoft Office suites. I don't have a problem with what ArtCraft are doing. At least from a technical standpoint, it's a great achievement. I just wonder if my app would be best as a multimedia player or if separate applications are the way forward. You can view it on my Github. I am building it for myself, and it's set to run on Linux through Debian or Flatpak.

AI in the right hands, with the right motivation, can be used to genuinely change lives for the better. Data centres can be a good thing if they are used for things like data sovereignty. I can see a future where the internet splinters and becomes more regional or national. With the public pushing for more data sovereignty, and regardless of the US midterm elections, there will be a concerted move in Europe away from the United States towards building more Eurocentric applications, hardware and operating systems. In the UK, we have Ofcom, who regulate our television, radio, broadband and phone services. They started regulating the online space a few years ago. I think this should have been the case from the beginning. While you could argue the "British Internet" would have grown far slower, I would say that it would at least be on more legally sound ground, and initiatives would be in place to help push British services like UK social media platforms, video platforms or search engines, and maybe even a British operating system for your computer. I still think this can happen and honestly hope that it does. We are going through an unprecedented age where we are being manipulated by what we read and see online, and our habits on social media, gaming and pornography are affecting how we live our daily lives. We already have instances on the Fediverse through websites such as Mastodon. You can use the social media platform to create an invite-only and well-moderated space for your school, workplace or lamb shawarma fan club using the ActivityPub protocol in the Fediverse.
