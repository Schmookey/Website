Physical Media

UPDATED Fri 17 Apr 2026 22:40
2026-03-29 22:40
⏱ – min read 💬 0 mentions ❤️ 0 🔁 0 🔗 0 replies

For a while I’ve just been coasting along with movies and TV. I used to purchase plenty of DVDs – mainly Asian cinema like Oldboy, Battle Royale, I’m a Cyborg, But That’s OK. This was before streaming really took off. I would buy them specifically because I wanted to own a copy. Much like the only Blu-ray I’ve ever bought, Ashens and the Polybius Heist, I wanted to contribute to their Indiegogo. Good films I knew wouldn’t be so easy to just watch, and that was a way of doing so while getting something back. It is still wild to me that I’ve had a PS3 since it was released and yet only ever bought one Blu-ray. I guess the extra picture quality wasn’t really a major factor; the important thing was owning a film I wanted to watch whenever I felt like it.

It isn’t even like streaming is easier because, honestly, I’ve watched a handful of films on streaming lately. If I’m watching anything via streaming, it’s series or podcasts/vodcasts. I’ve tried too! I’ve taken Mubi, Klassiki and BFI up on streaming deals for annual subscriptions, but I haven’t kept up with them. In terms of other media, I will happily purchase and download digital files for music; there is no DRM, and if I really want to own it, I can buy the CD too. With books, I’m comfortable with eBooks, more than I ever expected. I feel that’s partly to do with being able to customise the look of the page to be more incentivising. I never show the overall number of pages, just pages in each chapter, and I use a bar graphic to show the percentage of the book I’ve read. It breaks down the process for me really well. With video games, I’m happy to play them on Steam, granted after a deep discount most of the time, and I love having GOG. Good Old Games is great because their games are DRM-free. On a few occasions, I have bought a game on Steam and, however long later, when it’s released on GOG, I’ll purchase it again.

Recently I’ve had a bit of a kick in purchasing DVD box sets of TV series, pretty much buying them second-hand. DVDs have been around for 30 years now. While DVD players have improved over the years, the discs still hold the same quality, so buying a box set of a show released in 2004 and one in 2024 isn’t really a big issue, albeit with the inherent risk of purchasing second-hand – maybe the previous owner hasn’t been so careful with the discs. The plan is to build up a core of shows I know I’ll keep coming back to time and again, and maybe rip as many of them as I can to a hard drive and build my own Plex server. When I think why – why now? What has changed? – my mind is drawn back to when PlayStation established dominance and there were chunky grey boxes everywhere. I would go around different car boot sales, second-hand video game shops and the like and pick up Sega Mega Drive games for a couple of pounds; even more so, those six-in-one cartridges were common, so you could purchase Alex Kidd and Flicky alongside Shinobi and Columns. During the lifecycle of the PS2, I would go around similar spaces and find old PS1 games, and eBay could be good for a job lot of about 30 games for £20.

With the advent of streaming and this whole “you’ll own nothing and like it” culture, it makes me really want to do the opposite. I do want to own some TV series. I want to build up an inexpensive core of DVDs while they are numerous enough. I want to be in control.

So far I’ve been trying to focus on British television. It’s my bread and butter, what I’m most used to. It would be nice to hear any thoughts on this. Do you collect physical media? Do you have a favourite place to shop? Are you streaming all the way?
