ListenBrainz

UPDATED Wed 25 March 2026 20:20
Wed 25 March 2026 20:20 BST
⏱ – min read 💬 0 ❤️ 0 🔁 0 🔗 0 replies

Ever since I can remember, I've always had a strong connection with music; I feel emotionally invested. I find it difficult just to have it on passively. I am that person who is constantly fiddling with their device to select songs: "Oh, this song reminds me of that song", "Oh, that band have popped back into my mind, I need to listen." I feel blessed to live in this era where we can carry around gigabytes of music with us in a small box, rather than having to designate time to listen at home or lumber around a massive boombox. Yes, I know Walkmans existed—I had those. However, you were having to carry around the devices, headphones, and your precious CDs or tapes.

Just before I was really coming of age with music, we had the whole Limewire, Napster, and the torrenting of music illegally. Now, I don't have an issue with torrenting; I happen to think it is a great way to be able to download content. Ultimately, the more people that like the program, song, or game, the more likely they are to seed. Seeding essentially means everyone has a small piece of the file; 20 seeders all having a small piece each makes it faster and easier for everyone to be able to download it, and no one centralised server is subsequently in control of all of the file. Pirating gave torrenting a bad name in the same way cryptocurrency gives blockchain technology a bad name today—guilt by association. I tend to use places like FOSS Torrents today as a great place to find and download free and open-source software; it's kept up to date and they rely purely on donations and Patreon—they seem to be the textbook definition of doing it for the love of the game.

I've tried different music services. I subscribed to Spotify back in the day when it was invite-only. Heck, they even followed me on "Elon's Mouthpiece". I was particular about my Last.fm scrobbling. I even had the offline version of the app on my iPod Classic. Oh, how I miss my iPod Classic! I still have it, but the battery died and I've not got round to getting it replaced. For a time, music was throwaway for me. I would find lots of good music, but it just wasn't the same, and honestly I think it's to do with the ease with which we can stream it. It certainly wasn't choice paralysis. I also started using MusicBrainz Picard to help me tag my music better—I'm such a metadata nerd. Nowadays, I primarily use YouTube Music. I already subscribe to their premium service because... screw ads, am I right!? Well, except the ones the creators also put in their videos as part of their content. They were doing the whole "subscribe and get ads anyway" thing before it was "cool". Either way, it doesn't make sense for me to have YouTube and Spotify. I tend to make good use of Bandcamp, though. I've really got back into the idea of ownership—or at least my idea of ownership of digital goods—and not just buying a licence to listen or watch. I will happily buy a physical album too. I've been buying up second-hand DVDs like Blackadder, Monty Python, and Being Human—shows I know I like and want to own physically. I have grand plans of finally setting up a server and making digital copies of all my DVDs, PS1, and PS2 games.

Going back to my point on being uptight about getting all my Last.fm listens, recently I decided to try and get back to logging my music again. I settled on using ListenBrainz; it's part of the MetaBrainz group I discussed earlier when I mentioned MusicBrainz Picard. I like the MusicBrainz project because it's open source—it relies on everybody pitching in. Their ListenBrainz team are open about their mission, and they work under the CC0 Creative Commons licence, which essentially puts as much of their work in the public domain as possible. If you scrobble your music with them, they can also give you a weekly playlist of 50 songs—not groundbreaking, I know. However, I have been genuinely impressed with the mix of music it provides: a great mix of classics and modern greats, plus it's been good at throwing in some tracks from left field. One minute you are enjoying Supertramp, and the next, you are bopping your head to Dernière Danse by Indila. As I use YouTube, it means playlists won't just move on to the next song—you need to manually play each song and actively listen. This suits me and my relationship with music. I've really been enjoying listening to music with more intention. A lot of services are opening up to AI music. I am all for AI helping you—at college we were taught Wikipedia can be a good tool as a starting point, but it's not the place to base your research on, simply because it's publicly generated and effectively anyone can edit an article. We were told of a student who did an assignment on libel law; however, it was all about one specific state in the USA and didn't refer at all to our country. WHOOPSIE!!!! AI can give you ideas or some technical help, but using it to effectively write your college paper, news article, or to block government grants for being too "DEI"—whatever that means—is just wild! Much like when a non-techie person buys the latest iPhone but only uses it to text people. They have all the gear but no idea. By all means use it, but it's on you to learn how to use it properly.

I saw that OpenAI are closing down Sora—and not a moment too soon, honestly. It's one thing making a funny video of Sam Altman falling down some steps; it's another when it's used by bad actors for propaganda to further their political agenda. "AI" will be a game-changer in Hollywood alongside actors, writers, and directors, but it won't take over from them. I'm really keen to keep on my journey and listen more actively to music. I'd also be keen to know of any streaming suggestions or to chat about music in general.
